01 · THE PLACE

WHERE THE CLOUDS
COME DOWN LOW.

Anini is the headquarters of Dibang Valley, sitting on a small plateau between the Dri and Mathun rivers. The district is known for rugged mountains, deep gorges, forests, waterfalls and high-altitude lakes.

The Idu Mishmi people are central to the character of the valley. This is a destination for travellers who want remoteness, landscape and a slower connection with the road.

03 · THE ROAD

THE LONG WAY
IS THE POINT.

DIBRUGARH→ROING→MAYUDIA→ANINI

Official Arunachal Tourism material lists the Dibrugarh–Roing–Mayudia–Anini circuit. The district administration says Anini is about 235 km from Roing by road, with road and helicopter connections. Allow generous buffer time for mountain roads and changing weather.

04 · PLAN SMART

WHEN TO GO.
WHAT TO KNOW.

OCTOBER – APRILDRIER WINDOW

Official Arunachal Tourism guidance identifies October–April as a generally favourable period. Conditions can still vary in the mountains.

JULY – SEPTEMBERMONSOON

Heavy rain can affect roads and access. Build extra buffer time and check current local conditions before departure.

WINTERCOLD & REMOTE

Colder weather and possible snow at higher elevations mean warm layers and flexible plans are important.

PERMITS: Arunachal Pradesh requires an Inner Line Permit for domestic visitors and the applicable Protected Area Permit for foreign visitors. Verify current requirements before travelling.
REMOTE-ROAD RULE: Treat the journey as part of the trip. Road conditions, weather and local access can change quickly, so avoid over-tight itineraries and check conditions locally.
